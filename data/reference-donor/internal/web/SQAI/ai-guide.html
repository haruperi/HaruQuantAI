The AI Assistant in StrategyQuant X

StrategyQuant X ships with a built-in AI assistant — a chat that doesn't just answer, it acts: it reads your databanks, explains and audits strategies, configures and runs projects, writes custom code, drives the user interface, and can research on its own while you sleep. This guide is the complete tour: what it is, what it can do, how to talk to it, and how to extend it with your own AI plugins.

Not just a language model

A smart co-worker that learns while it works

Most AI chats start from zero every time. This one has a home inside SQX and a continuous learning loop: it plans, acts on your real data, checks its own results — and when the job is done it keeps what was worth keeping. Tell it once how you judge a strategy and it judges that way in every later chat; reject an idea once and it will never silently re-test it; let it work out a tricky procedure and it turns that into a skill it can reuse tomorrow. Every session leaves it a little more capable — and everything it has learned is plain files you can open, correct or delete.

1.What is it?

The AI assistant is a chat panel inside StrategyQuant X, connected to the running application. Unlike a general chatbot, it works with your real data — it never guesses your numbers. When you ask about a strategy, it reads that strategy's actual rules and statistics; when you ask it to change a setting, it changes the real setting; when you ask for a report, it queries your real databank and writes the file.

It is built from four kinds of pieces, all of which you will meet in this guide:

Tools

Concrete operations the AI can perform in SQX — read a databank, run a project, edit a file, click a button. Around 60 of them, listed in section 8.

Skills

Step-by-step procedures for bigger jobs — "audit this project", "build a mean-reversion Builder setup". The AI picks the right one automatically, or you invoke it by name.

Agents

Specialist sub-assistants the AI can delegate to — a strategy explainer, a config validator, an adversarial reviewer. Several can run in parallel.

Plugins

Folders that package skills, agents, commands and rules together. SQX ships several — and you can create your own, even by just pasting your workflow into the chat.

Out of the box the assistant runs on StrategyQuant's own AI models, billed in AI credits — nothing to install or configure. Prefer full control? Plug in your own API key (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, a local model server…) and it bills that provider instead.

2.Under the hood: a self-evolving agent

This is not a chatbot that turns one question into one answer. The assistant runs on a full agent harness — the same architecture behind professional AI coding and research tools — with a continuous learning loop on top: it plans, acts, observes, and when the turn is over it keeps what was worth keeping. Every conversation can leave it a little more capable.

  • The agent loop. For every request the AI plans, calls tools, reads the results, and continues — round after round — until the job is actually done. Ask it to "build and verify a breakout project" and one message can mean dozens of tool calls: configure, run, wait, read the databank, check, correct, re-check.
  • Parallel tools. When one reasoning step needs several lookups, the tool calls run concurrently — reading five strategies takes one round, not five.
  • Multi-agent delegation. The main assistant can hand sub-tasks to specialist agents, each with its own instructions and its own (often restricted) toolset — and several agents run in parallel while the main assistant waits and combines their results. An adversarial reviewer checking a conclusion is a different mind than the one that produced it.
  • Loops. A turn can end by scheduling its own continuation — SQX wakes the assistant up and the next iteration starts by itself. That is the primitive behind overnight research loops (section 12): hypothesis → run → evaluate → journal → repeat, for hours, unattended.
  • Long-run budgets. Autonomous work runs inside configurable limits — a maximum number of tool rounds per turn and a per-tool time budget — so a loop can be ambitious without being unbounded.
  • Layered memory. Facts and preferences survive across conversations and restarts: a long-term MEMORY.md in every prompt, a daily fact ledger written for it automatically after turns, and a Markdown knowledge base of what was tried, what the verdict was and what was rejected (section 11).
  • Self-evolution. A procedure that worked — an analysis recipe, a multi-step SQX workflow, a script that took effort to get right — is saved by the assistant itself as a skill, security-scanned, and loaded on demand in later chats. Ask it to "save this as a skill", or let it decide.
  • Auto context management. Long conversations never blow up: older rounds are compacted into a structured summary (goals, state, findings), oversized tool output is moved to disk, the full history stays searchable.
  • Live trace + Stop. Every round, every tool call, every sub-agent step streams into the panel as it happens — with a turn timer, and a Stop button that ends it all gracefully.

Skills, rules and agents from AI plugins are scanned live on every message — drop a plugin folder in and it participates in the very next turn, no restart.

3.First steps

  1. Open the AI panel in StrategyQuant X (it can also expand to a full-page view; when the assistant switches you to a module — say, opens a strategy in AlgoWizard — the full-page chat docks back to the right side so you see the module).
  2. The home screen offers capability tiles — pick the one closest to what you want, or just start typing.
  3. Describe what you want in plain language, English or your own. No magic phrases needed — the assistant picks its tools by itself.
"Summarize the Final databank — how many strategies are profitable, and what's the spread of Return/DD?"
"Set the Builder to generate mean-reversion strategies on EURUSD H1 and start it."

While it works you see a live trace of every step — each tool call and its result — and a Stop button that cancels the turn at any time. The model picker in the status bar chooses which AI model answers; the default choice works well for everything.

Tip: type / in the input to open the command menu — every built-in command, installed skill and agent, with a description of each. It doubles as a catalog of what your installation can do. ↑↓ to choose, Tab/Enter to complete.

Text to Strategy

Market & Data Analysis

Analyze the market itself, not just your strategies: read OHLC bars on any timeframe, export the full series and run Python over it, pull in COT positioning — and let the analysis pick the strategy style to build.

"Show me the last 20 daily EURUSD bars as an OHLC table, then the H1 bars for the last week."A quick peek goes through read_bars directly — no export needed for a handful of bars.
"Export EURUSD H1 to CSV and analyze the market in Python: average daily range (ATR), volatility by hour of day, and the return distribution by day of week. Show the results visually and tell me what they imply for an intraday strategy."
"Analyze EURUSD on M1, H1 and D1 — identify market regimes, session and day-of-week edges, compare them with the available COT data and recommend a strategy type to build."

Real data analysis — Python + pandas built in

The assistant does not just read the numbers SQX already shows — it can compute its own. A full Python 3.13 runtime with numpy and pandas is bundled inside StrategyQuant (nothing to install, works offline). So questions the results tabs cannot answer — return correlation between strategies, day-of-week and hour-of-day edges, volatility regimes, custom robustness metrics — are answered by real computation on your data.

The pattern is always the same three steps, and the assistant handles all of them for you:

  1. Export the data through a tool. export_data_csv writes OHLC bars, get_strategy_trades(csv=true) writes the full closed-trade list, and databanks export to CSV too — all into a scratchpad the assistant can reach (internal/web/SQAI/state/scratch/ in the SQX data directory — session-scoped, cleaned up automatically). Files worth keeping — charts, exports — go to the durable session workspace (internal/web/SQAI/state/sessions/), which lives as long as the session does.
  2. Run Python over the CSV. run_python executes a script on the bundled runtime — pandas loads the CSV, does the group-bys, rolling windows, correlations and statistics, and prints the findings.
  3. Show the result. A short answer in chat, or — for anything substantial — a full-page AI Report with the KPIs, tables and charts.
"Analyze my Final databank. Check day-of-week dependency, hour-of-day dependency, volatility regimes and return correlation between the top strategies. Use Python and give me a visual report."
"Export EURUSD H1 and tell me in Python whether it is trending or mean-reverting right now, with a recommended strategy style."
"Are the returns of my two best strategies correlated? Compute it from their trade lists."

The bundled runtime is isolated from any Python you may have on your PC, and the numbers come from a fixed script — the same data gives the same answer every time. This is what the market-analyst plugin (regime analysis, trade-list dissection) is built on.

Attachments — paste a screenshot, drop a CSV

The chat takes files directly. Ctrl+V pastes a screenshot from the clipboard (or a file you copied in Explorer), and you can drag & drop any supported file onto the panel. A small chip appears above the input while the file uploads; send the message and the file goes with it.

  • Images (PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP — up to 5 MB): the assistant literally sees them. Paste an equity curve, a settings dialog, an error message — and ask about what is on the picture.
  • Data files (CSV, TSV, TXT, JSON, MD, LOG — up to 50 MB): saved into the session workspace, where the bundled Python + pandas opens them directly — upload a trade list or any external dataset and ask for a full analysis.
"[paste a screenshot] What is wrong with this equity curve?"
"[drop trades.csv] Analyze these trades — day-of-week edge, drawdown profile, and whether the last quarter degraded."

Attachments live in the conversation's own workspace and are deleted together with the session. Seeing images requires a vision-capable model (the default Claude models are).

Backtest a saved strategy — hand it an .sqx

Point the assistant at a strategy file and it backtests it. No project to set up, no databank to fill — one sentence, and a few seconds later the answer is a report card in the conversation with the equity curve and the numbers.

"Backtest user/strategies/Strategy 0.152.sqx and show me the report."

The same page renders for results you already have: "Show me the results of Strategy 42 in the Final databank" — or of an .sqx file — gives the identical report card (stats, equity curve) straight from what is stored, without re-running anything. Backtesting is for when you want fresh numbers; showing is for when you just want to look.

Below the equity curve, the card also shows the strategy's logic as pseudocode — the same readable rules the Source code tab prints, in the same code editor — so one card answers both "how does it do?" and "what does it actually trade?".

There are two ways to do it, and the assistant picks by what you asked for:

  • On the file's own settings (the default). The .sqx remembers the symbol, timeframe, period, spread, precision, engine, money management and trading options it was built with — they are replayed exactly. This answers "how does this strategy do?" Nothing in your projects is touched.
  • Through a project — the file is loaded into a project's input databank and that project's Retest runs on it, so it is tested under your settings: another market, your costs, your cross-checks. This answers "how does it hold up on my terms?" The strategy stays in the databank afterwards.

The same strategy legitimately scores differently under the two — the file above makes +2 482 on its own EURUSD H1 settings and loses on a Retester configured for GBPUSD H1. That gap is the point of the second form.

Backtests run in parallel: open a second conversation (New session) and start another one while the first is still going — each tab has its own run and its own report. A project run is the exception: one project runs one thing at a time, so a second retest aimed at the same project is refused with a clear message instead of stepping on the first.

"Backtest all four .sqx files in user/candidates and tell me which one has the best Return/DD."

AI Reports — full-page visual results

A big result deserves better than a wall of chat text. When an analysis produces something substantial — a market study, Python findings over a databank, a correlation matrix, a dashboard — the assistant creates an AI Report: a standalone page with KPIs, tables and charts. The conversation stays clean: it shows a compact card with a live preview of the report, and one click opens it full-page, using the whole window width. "Back to chat" returns exactly where you were; the card stays in the conversation, survives panel reloads and even an application restart, so you can reopen the report any time. Reports belong to their conversation — "New session" cleans them up.

"Analyze my databank: export the data, use Python to find patterns and create a visual report."
"Do a market regime analysis of EURUSD H1 and create a visual report with a recommended strategy style."
"Analyze the EURUSD H1 strategies from the Final databank — does their performance depend on market regime, day of week and volatility? Use Python and create an interactive report."

Reports render in a sandbox: no access to the application, your files or the network. Charts are inline SVG or Vue-powered — everything works offline.

7.Agents

An agent is a specialist sub-assistant with its own instructions and (often) a restricted toolset. The assistant delegates to agents on its own when a task calls for an independent pair of eyes — several can work in parallel, and their steps stream into the same trace, tagged with the agent's name. You can also delegate explicitly: /agent-name the task.

AgentPluginSpecialty
strategy-explainerstrategy-analyst Explain a single strategy's performance stats in plain language.
idea-fingerprintquant-research Reduce one strategy's rules to a compact, comparable fingerprint (direction, entry family, indicators, exit style, timeframe).
config-validatorstrategy-architect Validate a drafted project configuration against the settings reference before it is applied.
research-criticauto-research Adversarial reviewer — tries to refute a research conclusion before it enters the journal.
Why agents matter: a reviewer that wants to find mistakes catches what a single assistant reviewing its own work would miss. The research loop, for example, only journals a conclusion after the critic fails to knock it down.

9.Build your own AI plugin

Everything in sections 4–6 comes from AI plugins — plain folders under user/extend/AIPlugins/ in your SQX data directory. SQX ships several; adding your own needs no build, no restart, no registration. Drop a folder in and it is live on the next message.

The fast way: paste your protocol

If you have a trading protocol, research checklist or a long prompt you keep re-pasting — make it permanent:

/make-pluginThen paste the workflow. The assistant converts it into rules, a skill, agents and a /command, saved as a plugin — and it survives every new conversation from then on.

Customize the home screen

The capability tiles and their conversation starters are not hard-coded — they are a catalog in internal/web/SQAI/features/sqx/ in your SQX data directory, one file per language (en.json is the master, the other twelve are the same catalog translated), served to the chat as-is. Edit it freely: no build, no restart — reload the chat panel and your tiles are live. Add a tile for your own daily workflow, or trim the starters to the ones your team actually uses. A tile looks like this: 

{
  "id": "my-research",
  "icon": "bulb",
  "title": "My Research",
  "shortDescription": "My daily workflows, one click away.",
  "description": "The prompts I run every day, as one-click starters.",
  "backend": "sqai",
  "inputPlaceholder": "Describe the research to run…",
  "starters": [
    "Summarize the Final databank — how many strategies are profitable?",
    { "label": "Morning digest", "prompt": "Check all my running projects and give me one ranked digest" }
  ]
}

Add the object to the features array of en.json; to have it in another language too, add it to that language's file with the same id and the same starter keys — anything a language file leaves out is shown in English. icon is one of the chat's icon keys (wand, chat, monitor, chart, code, book, puzzle, package, bulb, compare) — any other value, an emoji for instance, renders as-is. A starter is either plain text (shown and sent as-is) or {label, prompt} — the label is shown on the chip, the prompt is what gets sent, so a chip can hide a long prompt or a /command. backend is sqai for the local agent (the model picker is shown) or n8n for the AlgoWizard cloud pipeline (no shipped tile uses it).

10.Your own tools — AI tool snippets

Skills teach the assistant a procedure; an AI tool gives it a new capability. It is a Java snippet like any other in SQX, except that once it compiles the assistant can call it exactly like a built-in tool — same tool list, same arguments, same result. The snippet runs inside StrategyQuant, so it reads live projects and databanks directly instead of going through an export.

Create one in the Code Editor — New → AI tool — or ask the assistant to write it for you. It lands in user/extend/Snippets/SQ/AITools/, and the shipped AITest sample is a working example you can strip down.

The four things that make it a tool

In the snippetWhat the assistant sees
The class name The tool name, derived automatically: SomeRiskReport becomes ext_some_risk_report. The ext_ prefix is always added, so a snippet can never shadow a built-in tool — and the name is never hand-picked.
@Description and @Help on the class The tool description — what the assistant reads when deciding whether this is the right tool for the job. Write it for a reader who does not know your code.
Public @Parameter fields The input schema. int, double, boolean and String are supported; each field's @Help becomes the argument description, and a field without a defaultValue becomes a required argument.
run() The answer — whatever String it returns is what the assistant receives.
@Description("Rank the strategies of every databank by one metric.")
public class AITest extends AITool {

    @Parameter(name="Project", defaultValue="")
    @Help("Name of the project to scan. Leave empty to scan every project.")
    public String project;

    @Parameter(name="Limit", defaultValue="5", minValue=1, maxValue=100, step=1)
    @Help("How many strategies to return per databank.")
    public int limit;

    @Override
    public String run() throws Exception {
        // projects(), project(name), databank(project, name), databankNames(project),
        // table(databank) and withStrategy(...) come from the AITool base class.
        return answer.toString();
    }
}

Compile it — the Compile button, or simply "compile my snippets" — and it is callable in the next message: the tool list is rebuilt every turn, so no restart is needed. Type /tools and it appears under Custom AI tools. Only an external MCP client attached to SQX (section 14) builds its list once at startup and therefore needs SQX restarted.

"Write me an AI tool that lists every strategy in the Ideas databank with fewer than 100 trades, then compile it and run it."

Two rules worth keeping

  • Return a compact summary, not a data dump. Everything the tool returns stays in the conversation for the rest of the session, and the output is capped at 32 KB — the same cap as run_python. Aggregate inside the tool and write bulk output to a file, returning its path.
  • Never recompute strategy statistics. The engine already computed them, so read them. table(databank) hands you the databank exactly as the Results tab shows it, and withStrategy(...) reaches one strategy's stats, orders or source under the proper lock.

A tool call is given 120 seconds; override timeoutSeconds() when yours legitimately needs longer. A snippet that fails to compile, throws or times out reports the error back to the assistant, which reads it and fixes the code — the same loop as any other snippet.

A chat window forgets; a research programme must not. The assistant has a persistent home — its workspace, internal/web/SQAI/workspace/ — and everything it keeps lives there as plain Markdown, in layers:

The knowledge base

It is plain Markdown in internal/web/SQAI/workspace/knowledge/trading-memory/ — readable in any editor, and a valid Obsidian vault, so [[wikilinks]] work: 

internal/web/SQAI/workspace/knowledge/trading-memory/

  LEDGER.md     append-only: one line per finished piece of work + verdict

The ledger is deliberately one line per item so it stays cheap to read whole; the detail sits in analyses/ and is opened only when it actually matters for the answer.

Two commands

CommandWhat it does
/recall <instrument, idea or theme> What do we already know about this? Searches the ledger, the linked analyses and the backlog, then answers in four parts: already tried (with the deciding number), rejected — do not repeat, open, or plainly nothing yet. It never starts new work; it reports the state of knowledge.
/journal [verdict or note] Writes down what was just established — the ledger line, an analysis note when there is more than one line worth keeping, the backlog update, and a refresh of the memory pointers. Only measured results from the session go in; an inconclusive outcome is recorded as INCONCLUSIVE together with what would settle it, never dressed up.

Run /recall before starting work and /journal after finishing it, and the assistant stops re-proposing the idea you rejected last month.

"/recall EURUSD breakout"
"/journal rejected — the edge disappears out of sample, PF 1.02 on 2024"

Editing it yourself

The knowledge base is yours, not a black box. The 🧠 AI Brain button in the chat panel header opens the assistant's whole persistent home: a map of what is kept where (MEMORY.md, the daily memory ledger, knowledge/ with this vault, the skills the assistant wrote for itself — drafts can be promoted or discarded there), and a Markdown editor with preview for every file. It is all plain Markdown on disk (internal/web/SQAI/workspace/), so any editor or an Obsidian vault works too. Correct a verdict, delete a note, add a constraint the assistant has never seen: the next answer uses it. Every save keeps a backup of the previous version.

12.Autonomous research

The assistant can work on its own schedule. A conversation turn may end with a scheduled wakeup — SQX itself starts the next turn when the time comes, so a research loop keeps going with the panel closed, and everything it did is in the trace when you come back.

"Start an overnight research loop on my EURUSD project — try to improve OOS Return/DD, journal every iteration, and report findings in the morning."
"Run these three projects, watch them, and give me one ranked digest when they all finish."

The shipped auto-research plugin runs a disciplined loop: form a hypothesis → configure and run a build → evaluate the databank on out-of-sample data → let the research-critic agent try to refute the conclusion → record it in a journal → decide the next iteration. A pending wakeup is always visible in the panel and can be cancelled with one click.

Give a loop explicit limits in the request itself — "at most 3 iterations", "stop by 7:00", "don't touch the 2023–2024 holdout". The assistant follows them, and the journal plus the step trace let you verify that it did.

13.Safety & control

  • You see everything. Every tool call and its result streams into the conversation as it happens; nothing runs invisibly. Stop cancels the turn at any point.
  • Files are sandboxed. The file tools reach only the SQX data folder. Anything outside needs your explicit, conversation-scoped grant: /allow-path D:\my\folder (add ro for read-only). "New chat" revokes all grants.
  • File changes are snapshotted. Every write and edit keeps a restorable snapshot — an operation log shows what changed, and a restore tool undoes it.
  • UI control is opt-in. The assistant can always see the screen and navigate, but clicking controls and running UI flows is locked until you type /drive-ui — the assistant cannot unlock this itself.
  • Billing is transparent. On the default gateway every answer shows its AI-credit usage; with your own API key, costs go to your provider account directly.
  • Shell commands are audit-logged, and autonomous wakeups are always visible and cancellable.
  • Learning is visible and reversible. Every skill the assistant writes for itself is security-scanned before it can load; the 🧠 AI Brain panel shows what it remembered and learned, lets you edit any of it and promote or discard skill drafts (skillsAutoPromote in sqai.config makes new skills wait for you).

14.Beyond the chat

The in-app chat is one of several doors to the same AI machinery:

Claude Code — the terminal

If you use Claude Code (Anthropic's CLI), launch it from the StrategyQuant install root: every AI plugin loads automatically — SQX generates the marketplace wiring on startup, no symlinks, no manual steps. Your skills and commands appear as /<plugin>:<name>. The terminal offers a bigger toolbox than the in-app chat (arbitrary shell, arbitrary files, its own sub-agents) and bills your Anthropic account.

MCP — connect any AI client to SQX

While SQX runs, it exposes its tools over the Model Context Protocol at http://localhost:<port>/mcp (the port SQX runs on — 8081 in a typical install). Any MCP client — Claude Code, Claude Desktop, and a growing list of others — can connect and drive StrategyQuant with the same tools the chat uses: 

claude mcp add sqx --transport http http://localhost:8081/mcp

For headless use without the GUI, sqcli -mcp starts the same tool server over stdio.

AI inside Results plugins

Custom Results plugins (your own tabs in the Results view) can ask the AI a one-shot question — "explain this strategy's principle" on a button click — through the plugin interface's AI_COMPLETE message, the same PostMessage API every other plugin request uses. The completion runs on the same model the chat is configured with, so billing follows your model choice automatically: the default gateway spends AI credits, your own API key bills your provider. 

window.parent.postMessage({
  type: 'AI_COMPLETE',
  requestId: 'explain-' + strategyName,               // optional, echoed back
  params: {
    prompt: 'Explain this strategy in plain language: ...',
    system: 'You are a concise trading assistant.'    // optional
  }
}, '*');

window.addEventListener('message', (event) => {
  if (event.data?.type !== 'AI_COMPLETE_RESPONSE') return;
  // event.data.data.success = the model's answer
  // event.data.data.error   = what went wrong
});

It is stateless — no history, no tools, one question, one answer — and it never blocks the chat. House rules for spending users' credits: call only on an explicit click (never automatically while browsing), cache the answer per strategy with an explicit "Regenerate", keep the prompt small, and label the button as a billed AI request. The shipped AI Strategy Summary plugin implements the full pattern; developer details in user/extend/ResultsPlugins/CLAUDE.md.

And you don't have to write any of this yourself — the assistant builds Results plugins for you (Custom Analysis Tabs & Code), including ones that call the AI:

"Create an analysis tab with an 'Explain with AI' button: when I click a strategy and press it, send the strategy's pseudocode and key stats to the AI (the AI_COMPLETE message) and show the explanation — cached per strategy, with a Regenerate button." The assistant writes the plugin, deploys it under user/extend/ResultsPlugins/ and reloads the Results view — following the credit rules above by design.

16.Worked examples, end to end

The index above is the vocabulary; this is the language — one sentence that sets off a chain the assistant walks by itself. Each example shows the prompt, the tools it reaches for, and what to expect.

1. Build overnight, judged by morning

"Start the GBPUSD H1 build, wait for it to finish, then show me the ten best by Return/DD and tell me what the top one actually does."

run_project → wait_for_project (loops until done) → query_databank(sortBy=Return/DD, limit=10) → get_strategy_source(pseudocode) → the analyze-strategy skill

The whole build-then-judge loop in one sentence. Expect the assistant to re-call wait_for_project every minute while the build runs — that is the design, not a stall.

2. Triage a run that is going nowhere

"The optimizer has been running two hours and accepted almost nothing. What's wrong?"

get_project_status (throughput, accepted vs rejected) → read_project_log → get_project_config (the criteria rejecting everything)

The three questions a human would ask, in the order a human would ask them. It can tell you "9 400 generated, 3 accepted, and your Ranking requires a profit factor above 2.5 on 60 trades" — a settings problem, not a build problem.

3. Change a setting, safely, and re-run only that stage

"Set the initial capital to 25 000 in the build task and re-run just that task."

apply_config_file(save) → get_project_config → set_task_config → run_project(mode=this-task-only)

Note the first step: a snapshot before the change — say "snapshot the config first" if you want it guaranteed. And the last: without a mode, a run restarts from task 1 and overwrites what the later tasks produced, so say which stage you mean.

4. Undo

"That made it worse — put the settings back the way they were."

apply_config_file(load) for settings · restore_file for a file · get_operation_log first if you are not sure what was touched

5. Did the retest survive?

"Compare Final and Retested — which strategies survived, and did their numbers hold up?"

query_databank(Final) → query_databank(Retested) → the model diffs the two tables

Ask for the columns you care about — a full databank of every column is a lot of context spent on numbers you did not ask about.

6. Summarize 500 at a glance

"Summarize the Final databank — how many of the 500 are profitable, and the spread of Return/DD, net profit and drawdown."

summarize_databank(columns=Return/DD,Net profit,Drawdown)

One call, one compact answer: the profitable/losing split and, per column, min / max / mean / median / quartiles — computed over the full set. Use query_databank when you want the individual rows (the top ten, say), and summarize_databank when you want the shape of the whole thing.

7. Give me the EA

"Take the best strategy in Final by Return/DD and give me the MQL5 for it."

query_databank(limit=1, sortBy=Return/DD) → get_strategy_source(format=engines) → get_strategy_source(format=…)

"Which platforms can you export to?" lists the engines available on your installation; write_file will save the generated code anywhere you name.

8. Do the winners have anything in common?

"Look at the top five in Final — do they all rely on the same indicators? Is that a red flag?"

query_databank(limit=5) → get_strategy_source(pseudocode) × 5 → synthesis

Five strategies that all long the same RSI cross are one idea with five parameter sets — and the equity curves will not tell you that.

9. Explain a strategy to someone who does not read code

"Explain what Strategy 3812 does, in plain language, and tell me honestly whether it's any good."

the analyze-strategy skill → get_strategy_source + get_strategy_stats

The skill loads itself from the description — you do not have to name it. It answers in the language you asked in.

10. Audit the assistant

"What have you changed in this project so far?"

get_operation_log

Every tool call is recorded with its arguments and outcome. Written content is recorded by size only, deliberately — an audit log that copies file contents would become a second place your secrets live.

11. Find the thing in the UI you cannot remember

"Where do I set the ranking method for the builder?" — then "Open it."

search_ui_actions → invoke_ui_action

For settings, prefer asking for the change directly (set_task_config): one call instead of a dozen clicks, and it can actually confirm the change landed. Searching and navigation always work; clicking beyond navigation needs /drive-ui (Safety).

12. Write me an indicator

"Create a custom indicator that averages the last N true ranges, compile it, and make it available as a building block."

create_snippet(type=BlocksIndicators) → edit_file → compile_snippets(file=…)

Compile errors come back structured (file, line, column), so the assistant fixes and retries until the build is green; a successful compile reloads the registries and the new block appears without a restart. The same flow covers signals, databank columns, custom stats and MM/MC/What-If methods — the sqx-snippets plugin carries one skill per snippet type (/make-indicator, /make-signal, /make-databank-column, …), so the assistant loads the right API guidance for each, and you can tune that guidance like any other plugin.

13. Leave it running and come back

"Start all three projects, wait for each, and give me a summary table of how many strategies each one accepted."

run_project × 3 → get_project_status / wait_for_project per project → one table

Worth knowing: the agent loop has a default ceiling of 40 tool rounds per turn. A long poll across three projects can reach it — if it stops early, say "keep going", or use a scheduled wakeup instead: "check back every 30 minutes".

17.Getting better answers

Small things that change the quality of the answer:

  • Name the project and databank the first time. After that "the same one" works.
  • Ask for the columns you want. "…just profit, DD and trade count" keeps a 40-column databank from filling the conversation with numbers you did not ask about.
  • Say "snapshot first" before a settings change if you want the rollback guaranteed.
  • Say which stage to run. "Re-run just the retest" beats "run it again", which restarts from task 1 and overwrites the later tasks' output.
  • Ask it to check its work. "Did that actually apply?" → it re-reads the config instead of trusting its own last call. Worth doing after anything that matters.
  • Ask in your own language. The reply comes back in the same one, and the tools are identical.

18.What it can't do yet

Honest limits, so you do not spend a turn discovering them:

  • Results tabs beyond the databank row — walk-forward periods and Monte Carlo confidence numbers are still unreachable. The trade list, the rules and the generated code are reachable (get_strategy_trades, get_strategy_source).
  • Creating things — databanks can be created, cleared, copied, moved and removed; projects can be created file-first (write the .cfx, import with open_project) and removed. What cannot be created in place is a task inside an existing project.
  • Hand-off between modules — copying/moving strategies into another project's databank is the hand-off to the Retester or portfolio modules; what it cannot do is press those modules' own buttons for you.
  • Exporting — it can hand you the platform code, generate source files from .sqx, export orders and data to MT4/MT5; what it cannot do is drive SQX's own export dialog.
  • Market data quality — it can list, read, export and update data; judging data quality is still on you.
  • Confirming UI work — UI actions report that they were dispatched, not that they worked. And a refused click when you have not typed /drive-ui is the gate working by design, not a failure.

These are roadmap items, not permanent gaps. If you ask for one, the assistant will tell you it can't rather than pretend — and point you at the manual way in SQX.

AI-generated output can contain mistakes. Always verify strategies, statistics and configuration changes before trading real money — the assistant makes verification easy (it shows every step), but the decisions are yours.
